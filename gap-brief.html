Understanding the Role of a SOC Analyst

Gap Brief | Nick Tsai

The Line

The job posting I selected is for a Security Operation Center (SOC) Analyst I at V2X in Orlando, Florida. One line from the posting that stood out to me was that the position involves “continuous 24×7×365 monitoring, analysis, and response to cyber threats.”

Before watching the event, I understood the general idea behind this responsibility, but I do not think I could have fully explained what it would look like in an actual job. I assumed monitoring mostly meant watching security alerts, looking for suspicious activity, and reporting serious alerts to someone with more experience. I understood what tools such as a SIEM were used for from my cybersecurity coursework, but I did not completely understand the decision-making that happens after an alert appears.

The Event and the Speaker

Event: What Does a SOC Analyst Do? | Cybersecurity Career Series
Host: Infosec Institute
Speaker: Mark Viglione
Date attended: September 30, 2026
Event link: Infosec Institute

The event I watched was What Does a SOC Analyst Do? | Cybersecurity Career Series, hosted by Infosec Institute. The video features cybersecurity professional Mark Viglione and is available through Infosec's Cybersecurity Career Series.

Viglione graduated from Penn State University and has worked as a cybersecurity engineer for a Fortune 500 company. He also founded Enigma Networkz, a cybersecurity data analytics company designed to help small and midsized organizations protect themselves from cyber threats. He has earned certifications including the SSCP, GIAC Certified Intrusion Analyst (GCIA), and CompTIA CySA+. He has also developed cybersecurity courses and labs.

His career path is different from mine because he already has years of professional cybersecurity experience, while I am currently completing my degree and preparing to enter the field. However, his discussion was useful to me because he explains that SOC analyst positions can be entry-level or junior positions. That makes the role more relevant to where I am in my own career.

What Changed

The first moment that changed my understanding happened at approximately 0:32. Viglione explains that SOC analysts spend a lot of time monitoring different tools and alerts, but an important part of their job is determining what is important and what is not. This made me realize that monitoring is not as passive as I originally thought. An analyst is not simply waiting for an alert and forwarding it. They have to look at the available information and decide whether an alert deserves additional investigation.

Another important moment occurs around 3:29, when the discussion turns to the skills needed to succeed as a SOC analyst. What stood out to me was that the job requires more than technical knowledge. Analysts also have to communicate what they find to other people. Before watching the video, I mainly associated SOC work with technical skills. Now I understand that identifying a problem is only part of the job. An analyst also needs to explain what happened and communicate that information clearly enough for other people to act on it.

The third moment was around 4:38, when Viglione discusses the tools SOC analysts use. The discussion includes SIEM systems, intrusion detection systems, and tools such as Splunk and Suricata. This connected directly to the V2X posting because the position specifically requires analysts to analyze and correlate audit records using SIEM tools. It helped me connect tools I have learned about in school with what an analyst would actually use them for on the job. Instead of thinking of SIEM as simply software that collects logs, I now understand that the analyst has to use those logs and alerts to investigate activity and make decisions.

Speaker, Pushback, and My Question

One point I would push back on slightly is how SOC analyst work is described as an entry-level or junior path. I understand why Viglione describes it that way, especially because there are different tiers within a SOC. However, looking at an actual SOC Analyst I posting shows that even an entry-level position can expect a large amount of knowledge. The V2X position includes incident response, vulnerability scanning, SIEM analysis, continuous monitoring, security testing, and knowledge of several cybersecurity tools and frameworks. The posting does allow a bachelor's degree with zero years of relevant experience, so it is technically accessible to a new graduate, but there is still a large difference between being eligible for the position and being completely prepared to perform every responsibility on the first day.

If I could ask Viglione one question, I would ask: For a college student trying to get their first SOC analyst position, what two or three hands-on skills would you prioritize before graduation to show an employer that you can actually investigate an alert instead of only understanding cybersecurity concepts?

I would ask this because he mentions around 6:48 that students can build a home lab and practice using security tools before they have professional experience. Knowing which specific skills employers value most would help me focus my preparation instead of trying to learn every cybersecurity tool at once.

My Answer Now

If I were asked in an interview what “continuous monitoring, analysis, and response to cyber threats” means, my answer would be:

Continuous monitoring is more than watching a dashboard and waiting for an alert. A SOC analyst has to monitor security information coming from different systems, use tools such as a SIEM to review and correlate logs, and determine which alerts actually require investigation. Once something suspicious is identified, the analyst needs to gather more information, determine the severity of the situation, document what they found, and follow the organization's incident-response procedures. They also have to communicate their findings clearly to other members of the security or IT team.

I have not worked professionally in a SOC yet, so I would not claim that I could immediately handle every type of incident independently. However, my cybersecurity coursework has given me experience with areas such as Windows and Linux security, firewall configuration, access controls, network security, and system hardening. The next step for me would be gaining more hands-on experience with SIEM tools and practicing the process of investigating alerts from beginning to end. After researching the position and listening to Viglione, I have a much better understanding of what the responsibility means in practice and what I still need to develop before entering a SOC role.
